Why Traditional Scrum Falters with AI

Traditional Software

Follows a deterministic path. You write code to execute specific, predictable logic. The outcome is known.

AI & Machine Learning

Follows a probabilistic path. You run experiments on data. The outcome is uncertain and requires discovery.

The Core Mindset Shift

The fundamental change is moving from building features to running experiments. Work is framed as hypotheses to be tested, not tasks to be completed. The goal is validated learning, which then informs what to build.

FROM: User Story

"As a user, I want a button that recommends products so I can find items I like."

TO: Hypothesis

"We believe we can increase user engagement by 15% if we show personalized recommendations based on purchase history."

Adapting the Scrum Ceremonies

Sprint Planning

Focus on the research question and hypothesis. Plan for data exploration, feature engineering, and model training tasks. Acknowledge that the output is a learning, not a guaranteed feature.

Daily Stand-up

Discuss experimental results, data pipeline issues, and model performance metrics. Blockers are often related to data access, quality, or computational resources.

Sprint Review

The demo is about insights, not just a UI. Show model performance dashboards, visualizations of results, and what was learned from the experiment, even if the hypothesis failed.

Sprint Retrospective

Reflect on the research process. Was the experimental design sound? Was the data sufficient? How can we improve our model iteration speed?

The AI Scrum Cycle

Key Takeaways

Adapting Scrum for AI is about embracing a scientific, experimental mindset.

  • Embrace Uncertainty: Plan for discovery, not just delivery.
  • Redefine "Done": A completed experiment and validated learning are valuable outcomes.
  • Data is Paramount: Treat data pipelines and quality as first-class citizens in your planning.
  • Focus the Team: Use a cross-functional team of data scientists, ML engineers, and software developers.
