Organizational Change Management

Classic Theories for Navigating Transformation

Why is Change So Hard?

Organizational change often fails not because the new idea is bad, but because the human element is ignored. People naturally resist disruption to their routines and status quo. Change management theories provide a structured approach to manage this human side of transformation.

Lewin's 3-Stage Model (1940s)

One of the earliest and most fundamental models, viewing change as a transition between three distinct states.

1. Unfreeze

Preparing the organization for change by breaking down the existing status quo and creating a sense of urgency.

→

2. Change

The transition itself, where new behaviors, processes, and ways of thinking are implemented. Communication is key here.

→

3. Refreeze

Solidifying the new state by anchoring the changes in the culture, supported by new policies and reward systems.

Kotter's 8-Step Process (1995)

A more detailed, actionable framework that builds on Lewin's model, providing a step-by-step guide for leaders.

Phase 1: Create a Climate for Change
1. Create Urgency
2. Form a Powerful Coalition
3. Create a Vision for Change
Phase 2: Engage & Enable the Organization
4. Communicate the Vision
5. Empower Broad-Based Action
6. Generate Short-Term Wins
Phase 3: Implement & Sustain
7. Consolidate Gains
8. Anchor New Approaches in the Culture

The Prosci ADKAR® Model

Unlike the previous models, ADKAR focuses on the individual's journey through change. It states that successful organizational change only happens when each person goes through five key stages.

Awareness of the need for change.
Desire to participate and support the change.
Knowledge on how to change.
Ability to implement required skills and behaviors.
Reinforcement to sustain the change.

A Toolkit, Not a Dogma

These theories are not mutually exclusive. A successful change leader uses them as a toolkit. Lewin provides the high-level mindset, Kotter offers a leader's project plan, and ADKAR provides a framework for ensuring individual employees are supported through the transition. Together, they provide a powerful, multi-layered approach to managing change.
