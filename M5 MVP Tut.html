Minimum Viable Product (MVP)

Learning the Most by Building the Least

Use ← and → arrow keys or the on-screen buttons to navigate.

What is an MVP?

An MVP is not just a smaller version of your final product. It is the version of a new product that allows a team to collect the maximum amount of validated learning about customers with the least amount of effort.

It's about delivering value from day one.

The Core Purpose: Test a Hypothesis

The MVP is a scientific tool. Its primary goal is not to gain customers, but to test a fundamental business hypothesis and gather data to guide your next steps.

From Big Vision to Testable Hypothesis

Big Vision

"Build a platform to connect dog walkers with owners."

→

Core Hypothesis for MVP

"We believe dog owners in Brooklyn will pay for a simple text message-based service that connects them with a pre-vetted walker."

What Makes a Good MVP?

  • Viable: It must actually work. It should solve at least the core problem for the user, even if in a very basic way.
  • Valuable: It must deliver enough value that early adopters will use it and provide feedback.
  • Feasible: It must be buildable with minimal time, resources, and technical complexity.

What an MVP Is NOT

Just a Few Features

An MVP is not about shipping the first three features on your roadmap. It's about shipping the smallest cohesive product that tests your core assumption.

A Buggy Product

Minimum does not mean low quality. An MVP should be well-crafted and reliable. "Viable" means it has to work.

Phase One

An MVP is not the first step in a pre-defined roadmap. It's an experiment whose results will determine what—if anything—should be built next.

Build Less, Learn More

The MVP is a foundational concept in modern product development. It forces teams to confront their riskiest assumptions early, using real-world data instead of internal speculation to guide their path. By prioritizing learning over feature-building, it dramatically reduces waste and increases the chances of building a product people actually want.
