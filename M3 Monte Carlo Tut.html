What is a Monte Carlo Simulation?

It's a computational technique that uses repeated random sampling to model the probability of different outcomes in a process that cannot easily be predicted due to the intervention of random variables.

In simple terms: It's a way to see all the possible futures, not just the one you expect.

How It Works: A 4-Step Process

1

Model the System

Create a formula representing your outcome (e.g., Profit = Sales - Costs).

2

Identify Uncertainties

Determine which inputs are variables (e.g., unit sales, material costs).

3

Assign Distributions

For each variable, define its range of possible values (e.g., Sales are between 1k-1.5k units).

4

Run & Analyze

Run thousands of simulations with random inputs and analyze the distribution of results.

Example: Project Budgeting

Instead of fixed costs, we define ranges for our key variables.

  • Labor Costs: Best Case $150k, Most Likely $180k, Worst Case $250k
  • Material Costs: Uniformly between $80k and $110k
  • Contingency: 10% chance of a $50k unexpected event

The simulation runs 10,000 "what-if" scenarios to build a probability distribution of the total project cost.

Making Better Decisions

Monte Carlo simulations replace guesswork with a quantifiable range of possibilities. This allows you to make decisions based on risk tolerance, not just a single, fragile prediction.

  • Quantify Risk: Understand the probability of meeting your targets.
  • Identify Drivers: See which variables have the biggest impact on the outcome.
  • Increase Confidence: Make strategic choices backed by a robust analysis of potential outcomes.
