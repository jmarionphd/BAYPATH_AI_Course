What is a Community of Practice (CoP)?

A CoP is a group of people who share a passion or a concern for something they do, and who learn how to do it better as they interact regularly. It's an organic, social structure for collective learning.

Why an Innovation CoP?

While a traditional CoP might focus on improving existing skills, an Innovation CoP is a forward-looking engine for organizational change.

Cross-Pollinate Ideas

Break down departmental silos by connecting passionate people from across the organization to share diverse perspectives.

Build Capabilities

Create a space for members to collectively learn and master new innovation tools, methods (like Design Thinking), and technologies (like AI).

Accelerate Adoption

Develop a grassroots network of internal champions who can model new behaviors and help spread new ways of working.

Foster Culture

Make innovation a shared, voluntary responsibility, building a culture where curiosity and experimentation are encouraged.

The Three Pillars

A successful Community of Practice is built on three essential foundations that reinforce each other.

Activities that Fuel the Community

1

Regular Meetups

Scheduled, recurring events (e.g., monthly "Lunch & Learns") that create a reliable rhythm for the community.

2

Show & Tells / Demos

Members share recent work, interesting discoveries, or a new tool they're exploring in short, informal presentations.

3

Shared Resource Hub

A central, living repository for knowledge, such as a Slack channel, wiki page, or shared folder with articles, templates, and recordings.

4

Problem-Solving Sessions

A member brings a real-world challenge to the group for collaborative brainstorming and advice.

An Engine for Growth

Innovation Communities of Practice are more than just clubs; they are strategic assets. By connecting passionate practitioners, they create a powerful, self-sustaining engine for learning, capability-building, and cultural change that formal, top-down programs often struggle to achieve.
