What is the 70-20-10 Model?

It's a strategic framework for allocating resources (like time, budget, and talent) to balance maintaining your core business with exploring new opportunities for growth and transformation.

Breaking Down the Portfolio

70%

Core Initiatives

Incremental improvements to existing products, services, and processes. This is about running the current business efficiently and effectively. Low risk, predictable returns.

20%

Adjacent Initiatives

Expanding from the core into new areas, like launching a new product line for an existing market, or entering a new geography. Moderate risk.

10%

Transformational Initiatives

Creating breakthrough innovations for entirely new markets or customer needs. These are high-risk, high-reward bets that could define the future of the company.

Balancing Risk and Reward

The 70-20-10 model isn't just about budget allocation; it's a strategic approach to managing risk. The majority of resources are dedicated to safe, predictable "core" activities, while smaller, protected portions are allocated to higher-risk initiatives that hold the potential for massive future returns.

Why Use This Model?

Protects Future Growth

Ensures that long-term, transformational ideas receive funding and don't get starved by short-term needs.

Manages Risk Intelligently

Balances a portfolio of safe bets with higher-risk, higher-reward ventures.

Creates Strategic Alignment

Provides a simple, clear language for leaders to discuss and manage the company's innovation efforts.

Drives a Culture of Innovation

Gives teams permission and resources to work on new ideas, fostering a more experimental mindset.

Key Takeaways

The 70-20-10 model is a powerful guideline for sustainable growth.

  • Run the Business (70%): Dedicate most resources to your stable core.
  • Grow the Business (20%): Expand into adjacent areas to find new revenue.
  • Transform the Business (10%): Place small, smart bets on the future.
  • Note: These percentages are a starting point. Adjust them based on your industry, company stage, and strategic goals.
